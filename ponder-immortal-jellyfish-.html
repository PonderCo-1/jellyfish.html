Sources & research notes

This page lists the scientific papers and reports used to research the video. The sources support the jellyfish’s life-cycle reversal and describe molecular pathways that may be involved; they do not establish a way to make humans immortal. The page also lists a published critique of one study and the authors’ reply, plus mammalian reprogramming research mentioned for context.

For: “The Immortal Jellyfish: The Creature That Can Reverse Its Life Cycle”

Sources

Twenty-one references: fourteen research papers, one official erratum, two published letters (a critique and the authors’ reply), one Nobel Prize press release, one research database page, and two sources on the first human trial (a company page and a news report).

Comparative genomics and rejuvenation
  1. 01
    Comparative genomics of mortal and immortal cnidarians unveils novel keys behind rejuvenationPascual-Torner et al. · Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS), 2022. Compares the genomes of Turritopsis dohrnii and Turritopsis rubra and identifies candidate pathways associated with rejuvenation.
  2. 02
    Erratum: Comparative genomics of mortal and immortal cnidarians unveils novel keys behind rejuvenationPNAS, 2023, 120(14). Official erratum to the 2022 paper.
  3. 03
    On the perils of working on nonmodel organismsMiglietta · PNAS, 2023, 120(11). A published critique of the 2022 genome comparison. It argues that the comparison species may be able to rejuvenate, that the methods used to trigger the reversal differed, and that species identification in this genus is difficult.
  4. 04
    Reply to Miglietta: Toward doing rigorous and innovative science with nonmodel organismsPascual-Torner & Quesada · PNAS, 2023, 120(11). The genome study authors’ response to the critique. Both sides are listed so the disagreement is visible.
  5. 05
    Turritopsis Genome DataBase: AboutKyushu University research database page. States that life-cycle reversion has been observed in the lab in both Turritopsis rubra and Turritopsis dohrnii, including repeated cycles in a T. rubra colony from southern Japan. Relevant to the disagreement over the comparison species.
Life-cycle reversal and cellular changes
  1. 01
    Transcriptome Characterization of Reverse Development in Turritopsis dohrnii (Hydrozoa, Cnidaria)Matsumoto, Piraino & Miglietta · G3: Genes, Genomes, Genetics, 2019. Describes the medusa-to-cyst-to-polyp reversal and investigates biological networks potentially involved.
  2. 02
    Cellular Reprogramming and Immortality: Expression Profiling Reveals Putative Genes Involved in Turritopsis dohrnii’s Life Cycle ReversalMatsumoto & Miglietta · Genome Biology and Evolution, 2021. Analyzes gene-expression patterns across life-cycle stages and discusses candidate networks involved in reversal.
  3. 03
    Rejuvenation of senile medusa of Turritopsis sp. (Hydrozoa, Anthomedusae)Kubota · hosted by the Kyoto University research repository (journal and year not confirmed). Laboratory work on a Japanese Turritopsis species: needle-induced rejuvenation of old medusae was rare (10%, n=10), compared with 83.3% (n=12) in young medusae. Supports the “in some lab tests, old age” wording.
Genome assembly and transcriptomics
  1. 01
    Genome assembly and transcriptomic analyses of the repeatedly rejuvenating jellyfish Turritopsis dohrniiDNA Research, 2023. Provides a genome assembly and gene-expression data across developmental stages.
Life-cycle biology, distribution and mitochondrial genome
  1. 01
    Life cycle, morphology and medusa ontogenesis of Turritopsis dohrnii (Cnidaria: Hydrozoa)Martell, Piraino, Gravili & Boero · Italian Journal of Zoology, 2016. Documents normal life-cycle stages and medusa development, supporting accurate descriptions of the animal’s biology.
  2. 02
    Complete mitochondrial genome and evolutionary analysis of Turritopsis dohrnii, the “immortal” jellyfish with a reversible life-cycleLisenkova et al. · Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution, 2017. Reports the mitochondrial genome and comparative evolutionary analyses; it is a genetics reference, not proof of a single cause of rejuvenation.
  3. 03
    A silent invasionMiglietta & Lessios · Biological Invasions, 2009. Reports a shared genetic marker in Turritopsis dohrnii samples from Japan, Panama, Florida, Spain and Italy, and proposes human-mediated spread, most likely by ships.
First description of life-cycle reversal and species naming
  1. 01
    Reversing the life cycle: medusae transforming into polyps and cell transdifferentiation in Turritopsis nutricula (Cnidaria, Hydrozoa)Piraino, Boero, Aeschbach & Schmid · Biological Bulletin, 1996, 190:302–312. Early laboratory description of medusae reverting to polyps, including cell transdifferentiation. Published under the name T. nutricula.
  2. 02
    Species in the genus Turritopsis (Cnidaria, Hydrozoa): a molecular evaluationMiglietta, Piraino, Kubota & Schuchert · Journal of Zoological Systematics and Evolutionary Research, 2007. Molecular evaluation of species in the genus; relevant because older papers use different species names.
Reprogramming and aging research in mammals (context only)
  1. 01
    Induction of pluripotent stem cells from mouse embryonic and adult fibroblast cultures by defined factorsTakahashi & Yamanaka · Cell, 2006. Shows that a defined set of four factors can reprogram mouse cells to a pluripotent state.
  2. 02
    Induction of pluripotent stem cells from adult human fibroblasts by defined factorsTakahashi et al. · Cell, 2007. Extends the approach to human cells.
  3. 03
    The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2012: press releaseNobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet, 2012. Official announcement of the prize for reprogramming mature cells to pluripotency.
  4. 04
    Reprogramming in vivo produces teratomas and iPS cells with totipotency featuresAbad et al. · Nature, 2013. Reports tumor (teratoma) formation when reprogramming factors are expressed in living mice.
  5. 05
    In vivo amelioration of age-associated hallmarks by partial reprogrammingOcampo et al. · Cell, 2016. Short, cyclic reprogramming improved signs of aging and extended lifespan in a mouse model of premature aging. Mouse study; not a human treatment.
First human trial of partial reprogramming (context only; company-reported)
  1. 01
    ER-100 (epigenetic restoration): pipeline page, Life BiosciencesCompany page, not peer-reviewed. Describes the Phase 1 study of a partial-reprogramming therapy in optic neuropathies, designed to evaluate safety and tolerability. Claims are the company’s own.
  2. 02
    News report: first patient dosed in the ER-100 trialScienceAlert, 2026. Describes a small, safety-focused trial of up to 18 people and includes a skeptical expert comment. Used only for the statement that a first small human safety trial has begun.

A note on scientific wording

“Biologically immortal” refers to the jellyfish’s unusual ability to reverse its life cycle repeatedly under certain conditions. It does not mean the animal cannot die. Findings about DNA repair, telomeres, oxidative stress, stem-cell-related systems and cellular reprogramming are research leads or associations, not proof that one mechanism alone causes the reversal or that the same ability can be transferred to humans. The comparison between Turritopsis dohrnii and Turritopsis rubra is disputed in the literature; both the critique and the authors’ reply are listed. Most observations of life-cycle reversal come from laboratory animals; how often it occurs in the wild is not well established. Old age is named as a possible trigger in the literature, but the direct test cited here was on a related Japanese species and succeeded rarely. Reprogramming results in mice are early-stage and are not human treatments, and the first human trial mentioned is a small safety study reported mainly by the company running it. Early papers use the name Turritopsis nutricula; later work revised species identification in the genus. Images such as a “save point”, a “lock being opened”, cells taking off “uniforms” and a “rewind” are analogies, not scientific claims.
